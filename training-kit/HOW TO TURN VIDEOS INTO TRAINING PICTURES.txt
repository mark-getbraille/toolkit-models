How to turn videos into training pictures

Every step below starts with the word STEP, so you can find the next one with your screen reader's find command.

What this does
You film things while you talk about them, for example walking round a grocery store with Meta smart glasses
saying "these are Granny Smith apples, a dollar twenty-nine a pound". The kit takes sharp still pictures out of the
video and gives each one the words you said at that moment. The pictures and their descriptions go into a folder
that you can then choose as "your own pictures" for training. Your speech is turned into text on this computer;
nothing is sent anywhere.

The kit can also take still pictures from open-licence videos on the web, and from audio-described films you
already have. Those are explained further down.

PART 1: YOUR OWN VIDEOS

STEP 1: Film while you talk
- Say what is in front of you, in a normal voice, as you look at it: "this is the oat milk, the Oatly one",
  "bananas, sixty-nine cents a pound". Say the name on the label, the size and the price if you know them.
- Hold still for a second or two while you look at each thing, then speak. The kit keeps the sharpest picture
  near your words, and a picture taken while you turn your head is usually blurred.
- The kit uses what you say at the moment of the picture, or what you start saying up to 3 seconds after it. So
  say the description while you are still looking at the thing, not after you have moved on.
- Keep each video short (a few minutes). Quiet places work best; music and announcements make the speech harder
  to hear.
- Phone videos work too.

STEP 2: Get the videos onto the computer
- With Meta smart glasses, the videos are brought into the Meta AI app on your phone (the app used to be called
  Meta View). Open the Meta AI app, go to your glasses, and if there is an Import button next to Gallery, press it.
  On most phones the videos are imported by themselves (auto-import) and saved into the phone's own photos and
  videos (the Camera Roll on an iPhone, Gallery or Photos on Android). Clips that the glasses filmed by themselves
  (auto-capture) stay in the app until you save them to the phone yourself.
  Careful: deleting a video in the Meta AI app also deletes it from the phone's photos, and the other way round.
- Copy the videos from the phone to the computer: connect the phone with its USB cable and copy them from the
  phone's DCIM folder in File Explorer, or use OneDrive (or iCloud for Windows) to upload them from the phone and
  then copy them from the OneDrive folder on the computer.
- Put the videos in one folder, for example D:\Shop Videos.

STEP 3: Make the pictures
- In the program (where it offers videos), choose the folder of videos and a folder for the pictures. Or, in a
  Command Prompt in the kit folder:
      env\Scripts\python.exe kit\video_frames.py "D:\Shop Videos" --out "D:\Shop Pictures" --blur-faces
  (The first time, the extra tools are installed with:
      tools\uv.exe pip install --python env\Scripts\python.exe -r kit\requirements-video.txt
  and the speech recogniser, about 1.6 GB, is downloaded once.)
- Choices:
  --every 2       at most one picture every 2 seconds (the default).
  --max 50        at most 50 pictures from each video.
  --blur-faces    blur the faces of people in the pictures. Use it whenever other shoppers may be in a video.
  --language fr   the language you speak, if it is not English.
  --no-speech     do not use your speech (every picture then needs a description written by hand).
  --draft-descriptions   let the computer write drafts for pictures with nothing said near them (see STEP 5).
  --overlays crop        remove a date stamp or a TV station logo (see "Date stamps and station logos" below).
- It says what it does for each video and, at the end, how many pictures it kept, how many have your words, how
  many do not, and how many faces it blurred.
- A video that was done before is skipped, so descriptions you have corrected are never written over. To make a
  video's pictures again, delete its pictures first.

STEP 4: Check and fix the descriptions
- Each picture has a text file of the same name: Shop_01-12.5.jpg has Shop_01-12.5.txt. The name says where in the
  video the picture is: 1 minute 12.5 seconds.
- Open the .txt files in Notepad and read them. The speech recogniser can mishear ("the angry apples" for "these
  are green apples") and names of products are often written in small letters. Correct anything wrong and save.
  If a picture does not show what its words say (you had already turned away), change the words or delete both
  files.
- transcript.txt has everything that was heard in each video, with the times, if you want to look something up.
- "NO DESCRIPTION YET.txt" lists the pictures with nothing said near them. Those are not used for training until
  each has a .txt file. Write one in Notepad (same name as the picture, ending .txt), or delete the picture.

STEP 5: Drafts (only if you chose --draft-descriptions)
- For pictures with nothing said near them, the kit's own picture model writes a DRAFT into a file ending
  .draft.txt, for example Shop_01-12.5.draft.txt. Drafts are not used for training.
- A draft must be checked by a person who can see the picture: a sighted helper, or the describers on the Picture
  Describers website. When it is right (corrected if needed), rename it from .draft.txt to .txt.
- Why: training the model on its own guesses teaches it nothing new, and teaches it its own mistakes. Only words
  checked by a person make it better.
- Drafts need the NVIDIA graphics card, and the first time the picture model is downloaded (about 9 GB) unless the
  kit has it already.

STEP 6: Train
- Choose the pictures folder as your own pictures in the program (or train.py --own "D:\Shop Pictures").

Privacy and personal use
- Do not film people without asking them. In shops other people will be in the picture: use --blur-faces. It finds
  most faces seen from the front; faces seen from the side or half hidden can be missed, so a sighted helper should
  check pictures with people in them.
- Do not film card numbers, PIN pads, letters or screens with other people's details.
- Pictures from your own videos are for training your own personal model. Do not share them, or a model trained on
  them, unless everyone in them has agreed and you have the right to share what they show.

DATE STAMPS AND STATION LOGOS (for all the parts of this guide)

A camcorder can write the date and time into the picture, and recorded TV has the station's logo in a corner, or a
fixed news ticker along the bottom. These are burned into the picture, so the model would learn that they are part
of every scene. The kit can remove them from the stills.

STEP 1: Choose what to do with them
- In the program's "Take stills from videos" window, choose in "Date stamps and logos" (Alt+O): Leave them (as
  before), Crop them off, or Fill them in. The program remembers your choice. Or on the command line:
  --overlays crop     cut the edges with a stamp or logo off the stills. This is the cleanest. When cutting would
                      lose more than a fifth of the picture, the spots are filled in instead, and that is said.
  --overlays fill     paint over them with what is around them, keeping the whole picture.
  --overlay-box 80,85,18,10   also remove a box you give yourself: from the left, from the top, its width and its
                      height, all in percent of the picture. You can give more than one. Use it when the kit misses
                      a logo, or for a still camera (see the limits below). It needs --overlays crop or fill.

STEP 2: How the kit finds them
- Nothing is sent anywhere and no AI is used. The kit compares the pictures it looks at (about 4 a second): a
  stamp or a logo stays in the same place with the same outline while the picture behind it changes. Only marks
  near an edge or a corner are taken, each at most 8% of the picture.
- A date stamp whose seconds change is still found: its fixed digits mark where it is, and the whole stamp is
  removed.
- The camera has to move during the video. When most of the picture stays the same (a camera on a tripod, a
  still scene), the kit cannot tell a logo from the scene: it removes nothing and says so. Give the box yourself
  then with --overlay-box.

STEP 3: Check the result
- For each video, the kit says what it found and what it did, in plain words, for example "found 1 date stamp or
  logo (in the bottom right corner, about 3% of the picture). Cropped off: the stills keep 88% of the picture."
  The same lines, with each video's name, are written into OVERLAYS.txt in the pictures folder. The stills keep
  their usual names, so you still know which video and which moment each came from.
- Faces are blurred after the stamp or logo is removed, in the picture that is saved.

Limits
- A logo that moves, fades in and out, or is see-through may be missed, or only partly removed.
- Something that really is fixed in every shot near an edge, such as a car's dashboard filmed from inside, may be
  taken for a logo.
- Filled-in spots can look smudged, especially on detailed backgrounds. Crop is cleaner: use fill only when
  cropping would cut off too much.
- Have a sighted helper look at a few stills if it matters.

Whose footage it is
- For your own videos and for personal training only. Station logos and watermarks identify who owns the footage:
  don't share stills or a model made from someone else's footage with the logo removed.

PART 2: OPEN-LICENCE VIDEOS FROM THE WEB

STEP 1: Choose videos
- archive.org: the address of a video (https://archive.org/details/...) or just its name, for example
  AboutBan1935. The Prelinger Archives collection has thousands of public domain films.
- Wikimedia Commons: the address of a video file page (https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:....webm).
- NASA: the address of a video on images.nasa.gov (https://images.nasa.gov/details/...).
- Or let the kit search archive.org: --from-search "grocery store" --collection prelinger --max-videos 5.

STEP 2: The licence is checked for you
- Only public domain, CC0 and CC BY videos are used. Videos that do not allow commercial use (NC) or changes (ND)
  are refused, and so is a video whose licence is not known, with the reason said.
- Share-alike videos (CC BY-SA) are used only when you add --share-alike. Their stills are marked SHARE-ALIKE in
  CREDITS.txt, because anything made from them must be shared under the same licence.

STEP 3: Make the stills
- For example:
      env\Scripts\python.exe kit\video_frames.py https://archive.org/details/AboutBan1935 --out "D:\Web Stills" --max 20
- The video is not downloaded: the kit jumps to evenly spaced moments (20 from each video unless you give --max)
  and reads only a second around each one.
- CREDITS.txt lists each video's title, maker, licence and address, and the time of every still.
- These stills get no description, because a film's narration rarely says what is in the picture. They are listed
  in stills-for-describers.jsonl, with their credits, to be described by people on the Picture Describers website.
- --speech writes the narration near each still into a file ending .narration.txt, only for reference. Never
  rename it to .txt without checking that it really describes the picture. --speech reads the whole sound track,
  so the whole video is fetched.
- Keep web stills in a different folder from your own pictures.

PART 3: AUDIO-DESCRIBED FILMS YOU ALREADY HAVE (personal use only)

Audio description says what is on the screen in the pauses between the dialogue. Those sentences make good picture
descriptions. The kit only works with video files you already have on this computer: it does not download from
streaming services and does not unlock protected videos.

STEP 1: Find out whether a video has a description track
- Run the kit with --described: before anything else it lists every sound track of the video with its title and
  language, and says which one is marked as audio description. The same list is in the summary at the end.

STEP 2: Choose how the description is given (one of three)
- a) The video has a description sound track. The kit finds it by itself when it is marked (titles such as "Audio
  Description" or "Descriptive", or the "for visually impaired" mark). If it is not marked, choose it by its
  number from the list: --ad-track 2.
      env\Scripts\python.exe kit\video_frames.py "D:\Films\Film.mkv" --out "D:\Film Pictures" --described
- b) The description is a separate sound file: --ad-audio "D:\Films\Film description.mp3".
- c) The description is a text file (WebVTT .vtt or SubRip .srt subtitles with the description lines):
  --ad-text "D:\Films\Film descriptions.vtt". No speech recognition is needed then.
- When the description track is the whole film sound plus the narrator, the kit turns both tracks into text and
  keeps only the narrator's lines (the words that are not in the plain soundtrack at the same moment).

STEP 3: Check the result
- For each narrator line, the sharpest picture while the line is spoken is kept. Lines of fewer than 3 words and
  lines that are only a name are left out; pieces of one sentence are joined.
- Each picture's description is in a text file of the same name (the picture ending .jpg, its description ending
  .txt). Open it in Notepad to check and correct it. question.txt holds the question for these pictures,
  "Describe what is happening in this picture." Keep described films in their own folder, apart from your own
  videos (whose question is "What is this?").
- PERSONAL-USE.txt is written in the folder: these pictures and descriptions are for training your own personal
  model only. Do not share the pictures or descriptions, and do not publish a model trained on them, unless you
  have the rights.
