HOW TO DESCRIBE SEXUAL CONTENT, NUDITY AND CONSENT

Who this is for
Anyone writing or checking descriptions of nudity or sex for the picture describer: the people who describe pictures
in the Describe pictures window, and the people who write the answers of the packs (packs/adult_art_packs.py, and
training-kit/kit/adult_photo_review.py on the owner's computer). The describer is used by a blind adult who wants to
know what a picture shows, as a sighted adult would see it.

The rules
1. Say what is there, plainly. Use plain anatomical words: penis, vulva, vagina, breasts, buttocks, anus, pubic
   hair. Name the act plainly: sexual intercourse, oral sex, masturbation, kissing, touching. Do not use slang, and
   do not hide behind vague words ("an intimate moment", "private parts") when the picture is clear.
2. Say who does what. How many people there are, their position (lying, kneeling, seated, standing, from behind,
   on top), who is touching whom and where, what each person's face and body show.
3. Say what you can see about consent, plainly and as fact. Signs of willing participation (both reaching for each
   other, smiling, embracing) and signs of distress, force or restraint (pushing away, struggling, crying out, being
   held down, being tied, a weapon, being asleep or unconscious, being watched in secret). In a work of art that
   shows sexual violence, say so: "It depicts an assault; the woman is pushing him away." Name the story when the
   record names it ("the abduction of Persephone by Hades"). Never make force sound attractive.
4. No judgement and no titillation. Do not call a picture obscene, lewd, dirty, shameful, sexy, steamy, hot or
   seductive. Do not praise bodies or say what is arousing. Write as a museum guide would: calm, accurate, complete.
5. Age: say it only when it is documented. Write "an adult" or an age only when the record documents it (a stated
   age, a named person's birth date, "adult models"). Never guess an age from a body. Never describe anyone who is
   or may be under 18 in a sexual way: such a picture is not used at all, and must be deleted if you find one.
6. Art and photographs: say which it is ("a woodblock print", "an oil painting", "a photograph") and, for art, the
   artist, date and medium when known.
7. Nudity without sex: describe the pose, the setting and what is visible, plainly ("a standing nude woman, her
   weight on one leg, seen from the front"), without comment on the body's attractiveness.

Examples
  Good: "A Japanese woodblock print (shunga). A man and a woman lie on a futon having sexual intercourse, the woman
         on her back with her legs around him; both are smiling."
  Good: "An engraving of the abduction of the Sabine women: a Roman soldier carries a struggling woman, who pushes
         against his shoulders; it depicts force, not consent."
  Bad:  "A sexy, steamy scene of two lovers in a passionate embrace."  (judgement, titillation, nothing exact)
  Bad:  "A young girl ..." in any sexual picture.  (never: such a picture is deleted)

How the packs follow it
erotic_art answers come from the institution's own words, with crude and judgement words put plainly (PLAIN in
packs/adult_art_packs.py), and works of force, no consent or restraint get a factual sentence and a "theme" (so a
training can leave them out with --without-sexual-violence). The documented-adult photographs say how adulthood is
documented. The tests (packs/test_adult_art.py, training-kit/tests/test_adult_photo_review.py) check that no
judgement words are used.
