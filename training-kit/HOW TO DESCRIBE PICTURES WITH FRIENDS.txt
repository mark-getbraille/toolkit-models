How to describe pictures with friends

What this is for
You and your friends can write descriptions for pictures together, for example stills taken from your own videos,
and then teach the picture model with them. Your sighted friends can do most of the describing; you can describe
things you know, check their work and run the training. No website is needed: the work is passed between you as
zip files ("packages") on a USB stick, by email or on a shared drive. Nothing is sent anywhere by the program.

Personal use only: the pictures and descriptions are for training your own and your friends' models. Don't publish
the pictures, the descriptions or a model trained on them unless everyone involved agrees and owns the rights. Each
workspace and each package holds a file PERSONAL-USE.txt saying so.

Every step below starts with the word STEP, so you can find the next one with your screen reader's find command.

How it works, in short
A workspace is an ordinary folder. Each picture in it has its description in a text file of the same name
(kitchen.jpg and kitchen.txt), the same as Way A in "HOW TO ADD YOUR OWN PICTURES.txt". Beside them is one work
file, describing.json, that remembers for each picture its status, who described it and when, who checked it, and
notes. The statuses are:
- new: no description yet;
- described: someone wrote a description;
- checked: a second person read the description and confirmed it is right;
- needs another look: something must be looked at again, for example two people wrote different descriptions;
- skip: the picture is left out (blurred, nothing to see, or private).
Only described and checked pictures are used for training.

STEP 1: open the Describe pictures window
Start Picture Model Training (Picture-Model-Training.exe) and press Alt+J (Describe pictures). Press F1 in the
window at any time to open this guide.

STEP 2: make a workspace
Make an empty folder, for example D:\Describing. In the window, press Alt+B (Browse for the workspace folder) and
choose it. The program asks for the workspace's name (for example "Family videos"): press Alt+M (Make the
workspace). Then write your name in "Your name" (Alt+M in the main describing window). Your name is saved with
every description and check, and remembered for next time.
To open the same workspace another day, the program opens the last one by itself. You can also type a folder in
"Workspace folder" (Alt+F) and press Alt+W (Open or make the workspace).

STEP 3: put pictures in
- Your own photos: press Alt+A (Add pictures) and choose pictures, or press Cancel to choose a whole folder
  instead. The pictures are copied in, made at most 2,048 pixels on their longest side, and turned the right way
  up. A picture that is already in the workspace is skipped, even under another name. A picture with a .txt of the
  same name brings its description along.
- Stills from your videos: press Alt+V (Take stills from videos). Add the video files (Alt+A), choose how often to
  take a picture (one every 2 seconds at most, by default), whether to use what is said in the video as
  descriptions, the spoken language, and whether to blur people's faces. Then press Alt+T (Start taking stills).
  The first time, the program installs its video and speech tools into the kit folder (the training folder chosen
  in the main window); this can take half an hour. Each video is said as it is done. The stills go into the
  workspace; a still with your speech near it gets that speech as its description (check it!), and the others are
  "new". A video done before is skipped.
Every picture added is listed in Pictures with its status.

STEP 4: describe the pictures
Go to the Pictures list (Alt+U) and choose a picture with the arrow keys. The picture is shown, with its
description as its alternative text, and the program says its number, name, status and description. Then:
- "Question" (Alt+Q) is the question the description answers. It is usually "Describe this picture." You can ask
  something else, for example "What does the label say?".
- Write the description in "Description" (Alt+D).
- "Notes for each other" is for telling the others something, for example "Is this the blue mug or the green one?"
- Press Alt+S (Save and next) to save and go to the next picture. The cursor goes to Description, ready to write.
If Filter (Alt+R) is set to "Not described", only the pictures still to do are listed.

STEP 5: check each other's work
A second person reads each description and, when it is right, presses Alt+C (Mark as checked). If it is wrong,
correct it and then mark it as checked, or press Alt+N (Needs another look) and write why in Notes for each other.
Press Alt+K (Skip this picture) to leave a picture out. Set Filter to "Described" to go through the descriptions
waiting for a check.

STEP 6: pass the work back and forth
- To send pictures to a friend: press Alt+E (Export a package) and choose which pictures go in:
  "All", "Not described" (for a friend who will describe them), "Described" (described and checked ones), or "To
  check" (descriptions waiting for a check, and pictures needing another look). Then choose where to save the zip,
  for example on a USB stick. The program suggests a name with the workspace, the choice, your name and the date.
  Inside the zip is a README.txt that says what it is and how to open it.
- Your friend opens the program, makes or opens a workspace (STEP 2) and presses Alt+I (Import a package) and
  chooses the zip. Do not unpack the zip first.
- When they are done, they export a package the same way and send it back, and you import it.
Importing never loses anything. It says what it did, for example "12 new pictures, 30 descriptions added, 2
differences to look at." The rules:
- a picture you do not have yet is added (a different picture with a name you already use gets a new name);
- if only one of you wrote a description, it is kept;
- if you both wrote the same words, they are kept (and a check made by either of you counts);
- if one of you corrected the other's description, the correction is kept: the program remembers which
  descriptions each one replaced;
- if you really wrote different descriptions, the newer one is kept as the description, the other is kept beside
  it (for example kitchen.other-Anna.txt), and the picture is marked "needs another look".

STEP 7: settle the differences
Set Filter to "Needs another look". On each picture, press Alt+O (Show the other version). The window shows the
description in use, the other version (choose which one in "Which other version" when there are several), and a
field "Description to keep" (Alt+K). Press Alt+U to keep the description in use, Alt+O to keep the other version,
or edit "Description to keep" to merge the two and press Alt+E (Keep the edited description). The versions not
kept are moved into a hidden folder named ".resolved versions" in the workspace, so nothing is ever deleted. When
you send this picture back to your friend, their copy is settled too.

STEP 8: train with the workspace
Press Alt+T (Use this workspace for training). The window closes and the main window's "Your own pictures folder"
holds the workspace. Press Alt+K (Check my pictures) to hear how many pictures will be used: only the described
and checked ones; the rest are listed as not ready. Then train as described in "HOW TO ADD YOUR OWN PICTURES.txt"
(STEP 4 and STEP 5 there).

Keyboard commands in the Describe pictures window
- Control+S: save the description and stay on the picture.
- Alt+S: save and go to the next picture.
- Control+Page Down: the next picture; Control+Page Up (or Alt+P): the previous picture. What you wrote is kept.
- Moving in the Pictures list with the arrow keys also keeps what you wrote.
- Alt+F Workspace folder, Alt+B Browse, Alt+W Open or make the workspace, Alt+M Your name, Alt+R Filter, Alt+U
  Pictures, Alt+Q Question, Alt+D Description.
- Alt+C Mark as checked, Alt+N Needs another look, Alt+K Skip this picture, Alt+O Show the other version.
- Alt+A Add pictures, Alt+V Take stills from videos, Alt+E Export a package, Alt+I Import a package, Alt+T Use
  this workspace for training, Alt+H or F1 this guide, Alt+L or Escape close.
- The Status field says what happened last; the Progress field says where you are, for example "Picture 14 of 120.
  63 described, 20 checked." Long tasks (adding pictures, packages, videos) are said as they go, and everything is
  written into the "Picture Model Training logs" folder on the desktop.

Tips for good descriptions for blind people
- Most important first: say what the picture is in the first few words ("A tin of tomato soup on a shop shelf"),
  then the details.
- Colours: name them plainly (dark blue, pale green, bright red), also for clothes, food and packaging.
- Text in the picture: write out labels, signs, prices and screens word for word, and say where they are ("The
  label says: Heinz Cream of Tomato Soup, 400 grams.").
- Where things are: left, right, in front, behind, at the top, in the middle ("A mug on the left of the keyboard").
- People: what they are doing, how they are standing, their facial expression and clothes ("A woman in a red coat
  laughs and waves"). Name a person only if you know who it is and they agree.
- No guessing: describe only what you can see. If something cannot be made out, say so ("The price is too blurred
  to read."). Do not invent names, places or feelings.
- Keep it natural: whole sentences, no "image of" or "picture showing" at the start, no emojis.
- About 1 to 4 sentences for a simple picture; more for a busy one. Over 45 words is learned as a detailed
  description.
