How the Writing Kit handles private details

The kit has two privacy settings. Your rule is the default.

Setting 1: your rule (the default)
Every message, post, chat line, letter, comment and transcript is kept as it was released: the full text, people's
names, signatures, the earlier messages quoted in a reply, personal and sensitive subjects, and attachments.
Only these are masked, wherever they appear (in messages, attachments and transcripts):
- Social Security numbers (written like 123-45-6789, or 9 digits after "SSN" or "social security number"): [SSN]
- card numbers (13 to 19 digits in groups, or digits after the word "card"): [card number]
- bank, checking, savings, account, routing, IBAN, policy and member numbers named as such: [account number]
- passwords, passcodes, PINs and security codes given in the text ("password: tulip42!", "PIN 4471"): [password]
- login names and user IDs given in the text ("login: jsmith"): [login]
The word before the value stays, so the sentence still reads naturally: "my password is [password]".
Phone numbers, addresses, email addresses and names are not masked under this setting.
Why only these: they add nothing to how someone writes, but a model can learn text by heart and repeat it, and these
are what someone needs to steal an identity or get into an account.
How the government releases differ: email releases by government agencies (such as the State Department's Clinton
emails, or the Bush and Palin governor emails) were reviewed and redacted by the releasing agency before they were
published. The Enron emails were not redacted: FERC published them as they were. They are personal use only.
Two small things are still left out under this setting, because they are not writing: messages that are empty or
only a web link, and old Usenet binary files written into a post as text (uuencoded files).
A reply that quotes the message it answers is split: the quoted message goes into the instruction ("The message you
are answering: ...") and the writer's own words are the answer, so nothing is lost.

Setting 2: strict (add --privacy strict)
This is the careful scrubbing the kit started with. Every message goes through these steps:
1. Only the writer's own words are kept: quoted earlier messages, forwards and footers are cut off.
2. Messages about health, family and personal life, money trouble and banking, jobs and HR, anything sexual,
   children, passwords and legal matters, crime and police, gambling and drinking, or with swearing are dropped.
3. Email addresses, web addresses, phone numbers, ID, card and account numbers, dates of birth, street addresses,
   postcodes, IP addresses and long numbers are replaced: [email address], [phone number] and so on.
4. People's names become [Name], [Name 2] and so on, and the writer's own name [Your name]. Names are found from the
   message's own From, To and Cc lines, greetings, titles (Mr, Dr), introductions ("I am ..."), signatures, lists
   of common first names and surnames, and any capitalised word that is not an ordinary English word or a
   well-known place.
5. Only short, ordinary messages that read well are kept (about 12 to 200 words, not automatic, not a table).

The rules are in kit\scrub.py. The tests in tests\test_scrub.py and tests\test_more_sources.py try every rule on
made-up messages. No method catches everything: if you find a card number, password or Social Security number that
got through, tell the kit's maker so the rule can be fixed and a test added.
