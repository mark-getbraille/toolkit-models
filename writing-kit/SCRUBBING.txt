How the Writing Kit protects people's privacy

Email collections hold the details of ordinary people who never expected their messages to be read by a computer
program. So every message goes through these steps, in this order, before it can become a training example. The
rules are in kit\scrub.py, and the tests in tests\test_scrub.py try each rule on made-up messages.

Step 1. Only the writer's own words
Everything below the writer's own text is cut off: earlier messages quoted in a reply (lines that start with ">",
"-----Original Message-----", "On Monday ... wrote:", copied From and To lines), forwarded messages, and footers
such as "Sent from my iPhone" or confidentiality notices. Date and time stamps left by mail programs are removed,
and lines broken at about 75 characters are joined back into paragraphs.

Step 2. Sensitive messages are dropped whole
A message is not used at all if it, or its subject line, touches any of these:
- health and medical matters: doctors, hospitals, illness, medicines, therapy, pregnancy, injuries;
- family and personal life: husbands, wives, children, parents, weddings, divorce, funerals, adoption, romance;
- money trouble and personal banking: debt, loans, mortgages, salaries, pay, bank and card accounts, taxes;
- jobs and HR: performance reviews, firing, lay-offs, resignations, discipline, bonuses, hiring, interviews,
  recruiting, visas, pay stubs;
- anything sexual;
- children: kids, sons, daughters, babies, school, homework;
- passwords, confidential and legal matters: passwords, "confidential", "privileged", lawyers, courts, lawsuits;
- crime and police, weapons;
- gambling, drinking and partying;
- swearing and insults.
These lists are wide on purpose: it is better to lose a harmless message than to keep a private one.

Step 3. Personal details are replaced
Each of these is replaced with a word in square brackets:
- email addresses, including old Lotus Notes addresses: [email address]
- web addresses: [link]
- phone numbers in any common form, and extensions: [phone number]
- Social Security numbers and similar: [ID number]
- card numbers: [card number]
- account, order, invoice, ticket, policy and reference numbers: [account number]
- dates of birth: [date of birth]
- street addresses and post office boxes: [street address]
- ZIP codes and postcodes: [postcode]
- computer IP addresses: [IP address]
- any other number of six digits or more: [number]

Step 4. Names are replaced
Every person's name becomes [Name], then [Name 2], [Name 3] and so on; the same person keeps the same placeholder
all through the message. The writer's own name at the end becomes [Your name], and the job title, company and phone
lines under it are cut off. Public officials are not treated differently: everyone gets a placeholder.
Names are found in several ways at once:
- the names and addresses in the message's own From, To and Cc lines;
- the word after a greeting such as "Hi", "Dear" or "Good morning";
- the word after a title such as Mr, Ms, Dr or Senator;
- a list of common first names and surnames;
- any capitalised word that is not an ordinary English word and not a well-known place or company. This also
  replaces some company and place names. That is on purpose: losing a place name is better than keeping a surname.
Some first names are also ordinary words (Will, May, Bill, Mark, Dawn). These count as a name only where a name
fits: in the middle of a sentence, before a comma at the start of a line, or before words like "said" or "has".

Step 5. Only good, short, ordinary messages are kept
A message is dropped if it is:
- shorter than about 12 words or longer than about 200;
- an automatic message, newsletter, out-of-office reply or forward;
- mostly numbers, a table or a list;
- mostly capital letters, or written without capital letters at the start of sentences;
- not ordinary English sentences;
- so full of placeholders (more than about one word in eight) that it no longer reads as a message;
- only a note that sends an attachment;
- the same as a message already kept.

Attachments
Attachments are never downloaded, read or kept. The Enron file has none, multi-part messages are skipped, and on
the W3C pages only the message text is read, never the attachment links.

The writing datasets (OpenAssistant and Dolly)
These requests and answers were written for sharing, but the same steps 2 to 5 are applied to them, and to the
request as well as the answer.

What to do if something gets through
No method catches everything. Read samples.txt after a build. If you find a real name, address or private detail,
note the example and tell the kit's maker, so the rule that missed it can be fixed and a test added.
